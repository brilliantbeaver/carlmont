## Common Traps: Frequently Confused Pairs {#traps}

These are the mix-ups that multiple-choice tests are built to catch. Cover the right two columns and see whether you can explain each pair.

| # | Confused pair | How to tell them apart | Quick example | Review |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Mutualism vs. commensalism** | Does the *second* species benefit (+/+) or is it unaffected (+/0)? | bee & flower (+/+) vs. barnacle on a whale (+/0) | [[ref:s2-symbiosis]] |
| 2 | **Parasitism vs. predation** | Both +/−. A parasite lives on or in its host and feeds over time; a predator kills and eats its prey. | tick on a deer vs. puma eating a deer | [[ref:s2-symbiosis]] |
| 3 | **Intraspecific vs. interspecific competition** | *Intra* = within one species; *inter* = between different species. | gulls fighting over nesting rocks vs. lion & tiger over a buffalo | [[ref:s2-competition]] |
| 4 | **Keystone vs. most abundant species** | A keystone species has an outsized effect; it can be rare. | Atlas NP: 97 wolves vs. 7,000 deer — the wolves are keystone | [[ref:s2-keystone]] |
| 5 | **Which way food-web arrows point** | From the food → to the eater (the direction of energy flow). | redwood sorrel → mule deer | [[ref:s3-arrows]] |
| 6 | **Autotroph vs. heterotroph** | *Auto* = self-feeder (makes its own food, producer); *hetero* = other-feeder (consumer or decomposer). | redwood sorrel vs. brush rabbit | [[ref:s3-trophic]] |
| 7 | **Decomposer vs. primary consumer** | Decomposers break down dead matter from *every* level; primary consumers eat living producers. | fungi on a log vs. deer eating sorrel | [[ref:s3-decomposers]] |
| 8 | **Where the "lost" 90% goes** | It is used for metabolism and released as **heat** — it isn't destroyed and isn't recycled. | 10,000 kcal → 1,000 kcal | [[ref:s3-energy]] |
| 9 | **Energy vs. matter** | Energy **flows** one way and leaves as heat; matter (nutrients) **cycles**. | decomposers return nutrients to soil | [[ref:s3-decomposers]] |
| 10 | **Omnivore's trophic level** | An omnivore can be at more than one level, depending on what it eats. | black bear: level 2 eating sorrel, level 3 eating deer | [[ref:s3-trophic]] |
| 11 | **Density-dependent vs. density-independent** | Ask: does crowding make the effect stronger? Yes → dependent (disease, competition). No → independent (flood, cold front, fire). | disease in a crowded herd vs. a hurricane | [[ref:s4-density]] |
| 12 | **"Density-independent" = "no effect"?** | NO — it can kill many; the effect just doesn't depend on crowding. | a flood killing 25% at any density | [[ref:s4-density]] |
| 13 | **Exponential vs. logistic growth** | Exponential (J) keeps getting steeper; logistic (S) levels off at K. | bean years 0–3 vs. the whole class model | [[ref:s4-growth]] |
| 14 | **What raises vs. lowers K** | More resources or fewer threats → K goes up. Habitat loss, drought, or overgrazing → K goes down. | wildlife corridor (↑) vs. new subdivision (↓) | [[ref:s4-growth]] |
| 15 | **Overshoot vs. carrying capacity** | Overshoot = the peak *above* the K line; K = the level the population settles around. | bean sim: 73 (overshoot) vs. ~50 (K) | [[ref:s4-overshoot]] |
| 16 | **Predator vs. prey peaks** | Predator peaks come *after* prey peaks, and predators are fewer. | lynx peaks lag hare peaks | [[ref:s4-cycles]] |
| 17 | **Primary vs. secondary succession** | No soil → primary. Soil remains → secondary. A burned forest with soil is **secondary**. | Yosemite granite vs. Big Basin after the fire | [[ref:s5-secondary]] |
| 18 | **Secondary vs. tertiary (Ms. Hero)** | Tertiary = soil lost **and** humans step in to restore. | replanting an eroded, burned hillside | [[ref:s5-secondary]] |
| 19 | **Pioneer vs. climax** | Pioneer = first to arrive (lichens; grasses); climax = final, stable community. | lichen on rock vs. old-growth redwoods | [[ref:s5-organisms]] |
| 20 | **Crown vs. surface fire** | Crown = burns the treetops, kills most trees. Surface = burns the forest floor, big trees survive. | fire jumping tree to tree vs. burning needles and logs | [[ref:s5-fire]] |
| 21 | **Fire severity vs. intensity** | Severity = the ecosystem damage (effects); intensity = the heat released (behavior). | 77% high-severity burn in Big Basin | [[ref:s5-fire]] |
| 22 | **Fragmentation: amount vs. pattern** | The same total forest can be very different habitat depending on how it's broken up. | checkerboard vs. two big blocks (both 50% forest) | [[ref:s5-fragmentation]] |
| 23 | **Richness vs. evenness** | Richness = number of species; evenness = how equal their counts are. | Field X (even, 2 species) vs. Field Y (rich, uneven) | [[ref:s6-richeven]] |
| 24 | **Relative abundance formula** | (individuals of ONE species) ÷ (total individuals) — **not** number of species ÷ total. | 125 ÷ 200 = 0.625 | [[ref:s6-relabund]] |
| 25 | **Community vs. ecosystem** | Community = living things only; ecosystem = community + abiotic factors. | all Big Basin species vs. species + creek + sunlight | [[ref:s7-ecosystem]] |
| 26 | **Biotic vs. abiotic (dead things)** | Once-living things (logs, dead leaves) are **biotic**; rocks, water, sunlight, and weather are abiotic. | fallen redwood log = biotic | [[ref:s7-biotic]] |
| 27 | **IV vs. DV (DRY MIX)** | IV = what you change (x-axis); DV = what you measure (y-axis). Time → x-axis. | years (x) vs. rabbit population (y) | [[ref:s8-drymix]] |
| 28 | **Line vs. bar graph** | Line = change over time; bar = separate categories. | bean data → line graph | [[ref:s8-graphtypes]] |
| 29 | **Correlation vs. cause** | A trend on a graph doesn't prove what caused it. | more fires over time ≠ proof of one cause | [[ref:s8-reading]] |
| 30 | **Evidence vs. reasoning** | Evidence = the data (with units); reasoning = the science concept explaining why the data supports the claim. | "deer fell to 7,000" vs. "wolves are a keystone predator…" | [[ref:s9-cer]] |
